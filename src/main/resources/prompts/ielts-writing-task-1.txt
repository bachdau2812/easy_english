TASK ACHIEVEMENT

Assess how fully, appropriately, accurately, and relevantly the response fulfils the Task 1 requirements.

For charts, graphs, tables, and mixed data visuals:
- Check whether the response identifies what the visual represents accurately.
- Check for a clear and accurate overview of the main features.
- Judge whether the most important trends, contrasts, similarities, highs, lows, or other salient features are selected.
- Judge whether supporting data are used appropriately rather than mechanically listing every value.
- Check comparisons, categories, units, rankings, and directions of change against the authoritative visual analysis.
- Do not require every minor detail.

For process diagrams:
- Check coverage of major stages, sequence, inputs, outputs, transformations, and cyclical features where relevant.
- Penalize material sequencing or transformation errors.
- Do not reward speculation beyond the diagram.

For maps and plans:
- Check major spatial features and changes.
- Check additions, removals, replacements, expansions, relocations, and before/after comparisons where relevant.
- Penalize material spatial inaccuracies.

For mixed visuals:
- Check that important information from all relevant components is represented rather than effectively ignoring one component.

Overview discipline:
- A strong Task 1 response normally needs a clear overview of the main trends, differences, stages, developments, or most noticeable features.
- Do not require the literal word "Overall".
- Do not require the overview to appear in a fixed paragraph.
- Do not require a separate conclusion.
- A missing, unclear, materially incomplete, or inaccurate overview should affect Task Achievement according to its severity.

Factual grounding:
- Treat the authoritative visual analysis as ground truth.
- Distinguish major factual errors from minor imprecision.
- Accept reasonable approximations when the learner clearly signals approximation and the meaning remains faithful to the data.
- Identify material incorrect values, categories, units, comparisons, rankings, trends, or directions of change.
- For every reported factual error, provide a correction only when the authoritative visual analysis supports that correction.
- A factual/data error primarily affects Task Achievement. Do not automatically lower Lexical Resource or Grammatical Range and Accuracy unless the language itself independently has a lexical or grammatical problem.

Task 1 is an information-transfer task. Unsupported opinions, causal explanations, or speculation outside the supplied visual should not be rewarded.

The task requires at least 150 words. The application supplies the computed word count with the learner essay.
- Do not apply an arbitrary fixed numerical penalty solely for being under 150 words.
- Consider whether underlength causes insufficient task coverage or insufficient evidence of language range.
- Do not double-penalize the same consequence.
- Do not reward wording copied directly from the task statement as evidence of the learner's own lexical or grammatical ability.

COHERENCE AND COHESION

Assess logical organization and progression, grouping and sequencing of information, paragraphing, referencing and substitution, appropriate cohesive devices, and clarity of relationships within and between sentences and paragraphs.

Do not reward linking words simply because they are numerous. Mechanical, repetitive, inaccurate, excessive, or insufficient cohesion may limit the score. Do not impose a fixed paragraph template.

LEXICAL RESOURCE

Assess vocabulary range and flexibility, precision and appropriacy, collocation, word choice and word formation, spelling, repetition, and control of less common vocabulary where genuinely appropriate.

Natural, accurate, precise language is stronger evidence than forced sophistication. Distinguish isolated slips from recurring lexical weaknesses. Do not count a grammar problem as a lexical problem unless there is independently a vocabulary-related issue.

GRAMMATICAL RANGE AND ACCURACY

Assess both range and accuracy, including variety and control of sentence structures, clause construction and subordination, agreement, tense and aspect, articles, prepositions, pronouns, comparison structures, sentence boundaries, and punctuation.

Do not reward complexity for its own sake. Distinguish isolated slips, recurring patterns, systematic problems, and errors that impede communication. Do not manufacture grammar errors.
